01 — Why we wear pink

Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer in women worldwide. Found early, most people go on to live full, long lives. Pink Out is one day to make checking normal — to talk about it, look for it, and show up for everyone facing it.

02 — The numbers

Why every check counts.

  • 1 in 8 women in the U.S. will develop invasive breast cancer in their lifetime. American Cancer Society
  • 99% five-year relative survival when it’s found while still localised. American Cancer Society
  • 2.3M women were diagnosed worldwide in 2022 — each one someone’s person. World Health Organization

03 — Know the signs

Six changes worth a check.

Most changes aren’t cancer. But every change deserves a conversation with a doctor.

  1. 01

    A new lump

    In the breast or the underarm — even if it doesn’t hurt.

    ◍
  2. 02

    Size or shape

    A change in how one breast looks or sits compared to before.

    ◐
  3. 03

    Dimpling

    Skin that puckers, dimples or looks like orange peel.

    ⁘
  4. 04

    Nipple changes

    Turning inward, a new shape, or any unexpected discharge.

    ◉
  5. 05

    Redness or scaling

    Rash, flaky or thickened skin on the breast or nipple.

    ✺
  6. 06

    Pain that stays

    New, persistent pain in one spot that doesn’t go away.

    ✦

04 — Three checks

Small habits.
Big difference.

01

Know your normal

Look and feel, once a month. You’re not searching for cancer — you’re learning what’s usual for you, so a change stands out.

Monthly
02

Get screened

Mammograms find changes before they can be felt. Ask your doctor when you should start — for many women it’s every two years from age 40.

Ask your doctor
03

Speak up

Noticed something? Book the appointment. Know a family history? Share it. Then remind one person you love to do the same.

Today

05 — How to Pink Out

Four ways to show up.

  • Wear pink

    Pick a day this October. Shirt, scarf, laces, lipstick — the louder the better.

  • Host a Pink Out

    Turn a school game, office floor or team practice pink, and make it a talking point.

  • Share the signs

    Send the six signs to the group chat. One message can be the reminder someone needed.

  • Give

    Support a local breast cancer charity, screening program or patient support group.
