## Bugs found and fixed during QA

1. Hero poster didn't fill the hero (percentage height inside an auto grid row) → absolute positioning.
2. Hero first line was cherry instead of berry (specificity) → scoped selector.
3. 3D canvas was blank until the next animation frame after a resize → render immediately on resize.
4. Map labels sat above neighbouring zones → anchored to each zone's front edge; hint moved to the top.
5. Track headline squeezed at ~840px → buttons moved under the headline.
6. Review skipped the branch answers (filtered by the hidden step) → only fields hidden *inside* a step are skipped.
7. Stale cached modules → every internal import now carries the same `?v=` tag.

## v5: the partner walk-through (24 Oct 2026, client: "make it a journey; the partner should feel part of the event")

**What it is:** every partner page opens with a six-chapter scroll story told inside the 3D venue (`journey.js`, `journey3d.js`, `venue.js`). Content comes from `src/journey.json` (EN/AR, camera keyframes, sign position).

**How it plays:**
- The partner types their name; it stays in sessionStorage only.
- The name fills the chapter titles and a sign pinned in the venue:
  - event: the stage screen
  - beauty: a glam station
  - e-commerce: the market awning
- The name also pre-fills the application form.
- Visitors walk the floor and the partner's zone glows.
- `?walk=N` opens a page at chapter N.

**Fallbacks:**
- **No WebGL / Save-Data / low-power:** an illustrated story (render per chapter).
- **Reduced motion or motion off:** the camera cuts between chapters, and visitors stand still.

**Verified in the in-app browser:**
- personalisation (EN + Arabic name), chapter tracking and the sign at the right chapters, on all three pages
- phone layout (bottom cards, top dots), no overflow
- fallback layout
- home page unaffected

**Previews** (`docs/previews/walk-*.png`) were captured over the Chrome DevTools Protocol, because simple headless screenshots can't scroll.

**Fixed:**
- the new section first used the class `journey`, which clashed with the audience timeline, so it's renamed `walk`
- the camera path used arc length, so it didn't land on the chapter keyframes → `getPoint`
- the beauty and shop close-ups were too tight
- the keyboard skip link was visible
- fallback labels were white on white

**Not verified:**
- camera flight smoothness and visitor animation at full motion (the in-app pane runs as a hidden tab, so frames are paused)
- real phones / Safari

## v8 · 24 Oct 2026 · Event tour v2.1: render-style model, doors per passage, visitors with activities, world-by-world scroll

**Client feedback:** didn't like the textures and look; scrolling felt stuck halfway; visitors wandered everywhere doing nothing; visitors must come in through one passage and leave through the other; the single middle door was in the wrong place.
**Decisions (asked):** look = close to the concept renders; doors = one per passage (entrance door on the north end of the Entrance passage, exit door on the south end of the Exit passage, west wall solid); scroll = snap world by world.

**Built:**
- `venue.js` restyled after the renders: polished marble floors (canvas marble with veins and slab joints), light oak in Make and Move, white counters with gold trim, rounded fabric furniture (chairs, armchairs, sofas), glass shelves with products, flower walls and garlands, pendant lights, gold-framed mirrors; a studio light environment (PMREM) and ACES tone mapping so marble, gold and glass read; soft shadows on desktop.
- Each world furnished from the client's descriptions: Passage (arches + gold trim + garlands, mirrors, light-art panels, flower-wall photo spot with neon ring), Rose Hub (activation zone around the blossom tree with curved displays; oval café island with coffee and matcha sides; marble tables; two lounges), Beauty Lab (brand shelves, foundation counter, ring-light vanity, nail tables, fragrance bar, recliners), Make Studio (five stations with display walls), Pink Market (arched boutique bays, flower cart, gift display, ice-cream cart, jewellery case, pop-up kiosk), Move Studio (mirror wall, barre, mats, ball/weights rack), PinkOut Live (velvet stage, LED screen between arches with neon hearts, truss and lights, talk set, armchair audience).
- `crowd.js`: 122 activity spots defined with the furniture (sit, queue, browse, photo, yoga, dance, lead, watch, perform, stand); each resident moves the way the activity moves (the class bends and dances together, the audience claps now and then, queues shuffle). Visitors walk in through the north door and the Entrance passage, spend time in three worlds, and leave through the Exit passage and the south door. Instanced: two draw calls.
- `tour.js`: one stop per screen; one wheel gesture / swipe / arrow key = one world (trackpad flicks count once), animated camera flight between stops; outside the tour the page scrolls normally; the 3D module retries once if it fails to load.
- Phones: the venue fills the screen, the world card is a bottom sheet (scrolls inside when long), the stop rail sits at the top.

**Verified (in-app browser + headless Chrome):** every world renders in the new style; wheel test: entering from above lands on the overview, a 25-event flick moves exactly one world, up/down step correctly, the rail jumps; phone layout live (375 px); model size ≈ 520k vertices / ~220 draw calls, built in ≈ 0.5 s.

**Testing note:** the Python dev server drops module requests under parallel load, and software-rendered headless Chrome blocks WebGL after a heavy page; captures were taken with a fresh browser per shot and a small Node static server.

**Not verified:** frame rate on real phones (shadows are off on touch devices), Safari, the release from the tour at the finale with a real wheel (synthetic events can't scroll), Lighthouse.
